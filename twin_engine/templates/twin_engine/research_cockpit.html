What-if scenarios

Question answeredWhat simulated outcomes change when the intervention schedule changes?
Inputs usedBaseline/current recorded therapy, current twin state, simulated alternative intervention, toxicity constraints, solver outputs.
Method / computationA what-if scenario changes the intervention input and reruns the mechanistic model.
Output meaningRows compare model-estimated disease-control, healthy-compartment, durability, projected biomarker, and heuristic penalty terms.
What you can do nextOpen a report, compare trajectory curves, then read toxicity and causality panels before interpreting utility.
LimitationsUtility is a research ranking heuristic, not a treatment recommendation.
research_utility = tumor_reduction + (1 - healthy_loss) + durability_index - toxicity_constraint_penalty
research_utility_v2 = research_utility - 0.5 * liver_toxicity_signal_0_1 - 0.5 * neutropenia_signal_0_1
Metric definitions: Tumor reduction = model-estimated tumor burden reduction. Healthy loss = model-estimated loss in healthy compartment. Durability index = model-estimated persistence of response over horizon. Exposure columns summarize modeled schedule inputs. Prototype liver and neutropenia signals are normalized risk signals only, not AST/ALT or neutrophil predictions. Toxicity penalty = heuristic exposure penalty, not toxicity prediction. Research utility and utility v2 are heuristic research ranking scores, not treatment recommendations.

Method / computationMonte Carlo parameter perturbation, rolling-origin held-out prediction checks, one-at-a-time perturbation sweeps, and probability-best ranking under aligned uncertainty samples.
Output meaningThese are internal research diagnostics for stability, fit, and ranking fragility. They are not clinical validation and not treatment recommendations.
What you can do nextTrust narrower and more robust signals first, inspect unstable drivers, and treat wide-interval outputs as exploratory only.
